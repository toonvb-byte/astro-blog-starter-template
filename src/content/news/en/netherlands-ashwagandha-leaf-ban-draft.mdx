## What happened

The Netherlands has formally notified the European Commission of a draft decree on herbal preparations and food supplements (reported by NutraIngredients on 6 October 2026). The draft would:

- **ban ashwagandha (*Withania somnifera*) leaf** and preparations made from the leaf. Products made from the **root stay allowed**, with a warning against use in pregnancy, while breastfeeding and by people with liver or thyroid problems;
- **ban** *Huperzia serrata* and huperzine A;
- **cap black cohosh** at 40 mg a day;
- **require warnings** for garlic, green tea, black cohosh, turmeric, ginkgo, St John's wort, *Mucuna pruriens*, American ginseng, red sage, milk thistle and valerian.

The Commission and other EU countries can comment until **7 December 2026**. If the Netherlands then adopts it, the rules could apply from the end of 2026, with a six-month transition. The 2025 version would have banned ashwagandha outright; it drew more than 700 responses, most of them against.

## What it means for you

- **Check which part of the plant you are buying.** KSM-66 is a root-only extract; some other branded extracts combine root and leaf. Many labels just say "ashwagandha extract", which tells you nothing.
- **Outside the Netherlands nothing changes yet**, but several EU-wide online shops are based there, so their ranges may change.
- **In the UK** ashwagandha is legal as a food supplement. The FSA's Committee on Toxicity has been assessing its safety since 2024 and had not published a conclusion by October 2026; until then the FSA advises pregnant women and people with liver conditions to avoid it.
- Denmark remains the only European country with an outright ban in supplements.

We will check the outcome after 7 December and update our ashwagandha guide and supplement page.
