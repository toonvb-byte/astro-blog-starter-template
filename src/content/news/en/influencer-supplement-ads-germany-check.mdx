## What happened

Germany's consumer advice centres, the Verbraucherzentralen, published a market check of influencer marketing for food supplements on 9 November 2026. They looked at the Instagram and TikTok accounts of 27 fitness, beauty and family influencers: posts from January 2026 and stories from one week in May. Of 1,662 posts recorded, 804 were about supplements or other foods.

- **378 of the 804 posts (47%)** made health claims. Only **5** met the EU Health Claims Regulation, in the centres' assessment.
- **160 posts (20%)** gave the impression that a product could prevent, treat or cure a disease, which is banned for foods.
- **589 posts** were classed as advertising, and **none** was labelled correctly: 440 had no ad label at all and 149 an inadequate one.

Common tactics were fear of nutrient deficiency or pollutants, borrowed expertise, success stories and pressure to buy through discount codes. Claims and shop links were often spread over several posts, which makes them harder to judge legally. The centres want the claims rules applied to such post series, better-resourced food inspection and more responsibility for the platforms.

## What it means for you

- **The rules are the same in the UK.** Great Britain's claims register was copied from the EU register, and the Advertising Standards Authority applies it to ads and websites. In October the ASA found [1,383 rule-breaking ADHD and autism supplement ads](/en/news/asa-adhd-autism-supplement-ads/).
- **Real claims sound dull.** Magnesium, for example, may say it contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue. Our [table of authorised claims](/en/guides/eu-approved-health-claims-supplements/) shows what each popular ingredient may say.
- **Warning signs:** one product for many complaints, evidence only from testimonials, single studies or cell and animal research, ordinary food described as not enough, and a discount code without a clear ad label.
- **If you take medicines**, check with your GP or pharmacist before buying.
- **Report it:** take a screen recording first, because stories disappear after 24 hours. In the UK you can complain to the ASA; in Germany, to a Verbraucherzentrale.
