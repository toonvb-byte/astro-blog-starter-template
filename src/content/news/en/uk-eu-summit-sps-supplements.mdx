## What happened

The second UK-EU summit takes place in Brussels on **20 November 2026**. On 3 November the BBC reported that the two sides had agreed a **draft sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) agreement** of around 200 pages, which the EU sent to its member states for discussion on 4 November. Formal agreement is expected at the summit.

Under the deal the UK would follow EU animal, plant and food rules, including future changes ("dynamic alignment"). The government has announced a European Partnerships Bill to give new EU rules legal effect in the UK. Ministers still aim for **mid-2027**; FoodNavigator reports that the rollout may slip to early 2028. The Conservatives have said they would scrap any deal that requires dynamic alignment.

## What it means for you

The agreement covers much more than border checks. According to the government's list and legal analyses, it extends to food supplements, fortified foods, nutrition and health claims, additives, labelling and novel foods. The draft has not been published, and reports so far do not cover supplements. If it goes ahead as planned, for supplements sold in Great Britain:

- **Health claims** would follow the EU register again. The GB register started as a copy of the EU one in 2020, so most wording stays the same, but future EU and GB decisions would no longer drift apart.
- **Novel foods** would need EU authorisation; GB-only approvals would stop applying. Lawyers point to **CBD** foods and supplements, which have no EU authorisation so far, as the most exposed category.
- **Additive rules** would align: titanium dioxide (E171), still allowed in GB but banned in the EU and Northern Ireland, would be affected.
- **EU-wide maximum levels for vitamins and minerals**, once the EU sets them, would apply too. Today the UK has none in law.

Separately, the FSA is consulting until 16 November on authorising 16 novel foods for use in food and supplements in England, Wales and Northern Ireland.
