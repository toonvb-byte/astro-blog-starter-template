## What happened

At their summit in Brussels on **20 November 2026**, the UK and the EU agreed the sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) agreement. Both sides aim to apply it from **1 March 2028**, subject to their own procedures. In the UK that means a European Partnership Bill and secondary legislation.

From that date Great Britain follows EU food rules, including later EU changes. The government's list of what is covered names food supplements, fortified foods, nutrition and health claims and nutrition labelling. Until then current GB rules apply, and products already on the GB market can be sold until stocks run out or they reach the end of their shelf life.

The Food Standards Agency keeps its public-health role and gains access to EFSA's scientific work and to the EU food alert network (iRASFF).

## What it means for you

- **Nothing changes on the shelf yet.** Supplements sold in Great Britain follow GB rules until 1 March 2028.
- **More vitamin and mineral forms.** Forms the EU has added since Brexit may then be used in GB too, on a voluntary basis, according to the government guidance.
- **Health claims.** Claims the EU has authorised since Brexit, but GB has not, may then be used in GB. The guidance does not say what happens to GB's own claim decisions. Our [table of approved claims](/en/guides/eu-approved-health-claims-supplements/) shows both registers.
- **CBD.** GB novel food authorisations stop applying from March 2028. CBD products authorised in GB before then may stay on the GB market until 31 October 2029. For the EU position, see our item on the [EU court ruling on CBD](/en/news/eu-court-cbd-foods-no-organic-label/).
- **Titanium dioxide (E171).** The EU withdrew this white colour as a food additive in 2022; GB still allows it. From March 2028 EU additive authorisations apply in GB. Our [label guide](/en/guides/how-to-read-eu-supplement-label/) shows where to spot it.
- **Doses.** The guidance sets no maximum doses for vitamins or minerals. If the EU adopts common limits later, GB would follow them; see our [guide to maximum levels](/en/guides/supplement-maximum-levels-europe/).
