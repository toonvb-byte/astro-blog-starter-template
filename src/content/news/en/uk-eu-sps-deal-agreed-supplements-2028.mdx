## What happened

At their summit in Brussels on **20 November 2026**, the UK and the EU agreed the sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) agreement. Both sides aim to apply it from **1 March 2028**, subject to their own procedures. On **26 November 2026** the government introduced the European Partnership Bill in the House of Commons, so that ministers can apply the agreement through secondary legislation. A targeted consultation on changes to food and feed law is to follow.

From 1 March 2028 Great Britain follows EU food rules, including later EU changes. The government's list of what is covered names food supplements, fortified foods, nutrition and health claims and nutrition labelling. Products already on the GB market by then can be sold until stocks run out or their shelf life ends.

The Food Standards Agency (FSA) keeps its public-health role and gains access to EFSA's work and the EU food alert network (iRASFF).

## What it means for you

- **Nothing changes on the shelf yet.** Supplements sold in Great Britain follow GB rules until 1 March 2028.
- **More vitamin and mineral forms.** The government guidance says forms the EU has added since Brexit may then be used in GB too, voluntarily.
- **Health claims.** Claims the EU has authorised since Brexit, but GB has not, may then be used in GB. The guidance is silent on GB's own claim decisions. Our [table of approved claims](/en/guides/eu-approved-health-claims-supplements/) shows both registers.
- **CBD.** GB novel food authorisations stop applying in March 2028, but CBD products authorised in GB before then may stay on the GB market until 31 October 2029. Under current plans the FSA keeps working on CBD applications: it has consulted on three applications, covering around 3,000 products, and ten more have completed risk assessment. For the EU position, see our item on the [EU court ruling on CBD](/en/news/eu-court-cbd-foods-no-organic-label/).
- **Titanium dioxide (E171).** The EU withdrew this white colour in 2022; GB allows it until EU additive rules apply in March 2028. Our [label guide](/en/guides/how-to-read-eu-supplement-label/) shows where to spot it.
- **Doses.** The guidance sets no maximum doses for vitamins or minerals. If the EU adopts common limits later, GB would follow them; see our [guide to maximum levels](/en/guides/supplement-maximum-levels-europe/).
