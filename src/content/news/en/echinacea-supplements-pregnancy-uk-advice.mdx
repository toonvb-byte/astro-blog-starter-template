## What happened

On 9 November 2026 the UK Committee on Toxicity (COT), the independent expert committee that advises the Food Standards Agency on chemical safety, published a statement on echinacea in the maternal diet. It covered foods such as teas and honey and food supplements such as tablets, capsules and tinctures made from *Echinacea purpurea*, *E. angustifolia* or *E. pallida*, from before pregnancy to 24 months after birth. It is part of the committee's work on the maternal diet, which supports a review by the Scientific Advisory Committee on Nutrition.

Studies report that between 0.5% and 9.2% of pregnant women use echinacea products. The human studies found no evidence of harm, but they relied on women's own reports, and the dose, product and duration were often unknown. Some mouse studies hinted at more pregnancy losses; the committee judged these effects small and inconsistent, and studies in pigs and rabbits found no differences.

The committee concluded there is not enough reliable information to properly assess safety in pregnancy or while breastfeeding. Harm is considered unlikely with short-term use at the levels found in foods and supplements, but considerable uncertainty remains. As a precaution, pregnant women are advised to avoid echinacea food supplements unless a qualified healthcare professional recommends them.

## What it means for you

- **Pregnant?** Leave echinacea tablets, capsules and tinctures out unless your GP, midwife or pharmacist recommends one, and tell them about any herbal product you already take.
- **Breastfeeding:** the committee says safety is uncertain here too. Echinacea herbal medicines registered in the UK under the traditional herbal scheme are not recommended for pregnant or breastfeeding women.
- **Labels vary a lot.** The committee found that many products do not state the echinacea species or the amount per dose.
- **Echinacea has no authorised health claim** in Great Britain or the EU. See our [guide to immune support supplements](/en/guides/immune-support-supplements-winter/) for what the evidence shows.
