## The short answer

Neither zinc nor vitamin C is a shield against colds. **Daily vitamin C does not reduce how many colds you catch**, though it shortens them slightly. **Starting it once you are ill has no consistent effect.** **Zinc** may shorten a cold by about two days if taken early, but the evidence is **low certainty**. If you eat fruit and vegetables every day, you probably already get enough vitamin C.

## What the Cochrane reviews found

| Question | Vitamin C (Cochrane 2013) | Zinc (Cochrane 2024) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fewer colds? | No in the general population (10,000+ participants). Yes under extreme physical stress: risk halved in marathon runners, skiers and soldiers in subarctic exercises | No clear benefit for prevention |
| Shorter colds if taken daily? | 8% in adults, 14% in children | — |
| Worth starting at first symptoms? | No consistent effect in trials | May shorten by about 2 days (8 studies, 972 people), low certainty |
| Side effects | Doses of several grams can upset the stomach | Nausea, digestive upset, bad taste |

In context: a 7-day adult cold would be about **13 hours shorter** with daily vitamin C taken all year. The review's authors conclude that routine supplementation is not justified for the general population.

On zinc, the review's senior author said the evidence is "far from settled": trials use very different forms, doses and lozenges.

## What EU law allows

| Nutrient | Authorised claim | Minimum per daily dose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vitamin C | Contributes to the normal function of the immune system | 12 mg |
| Vitamin C | Contributes to maintaining normal immune function during and after intense physical exercise | 200 mg on top of normal intake |
| Zinc | Contributes to the normal function of the immune system | 1.5 mg |
| Vitamin D | Contributes to the normal function of the immune system | 0.75 µg |

None mentions **preventing, shortening or curing colds**. See our [table of EU-approved health claims](/en/guides/eu-approved-health-claims-supplements/).

## How much is too much

- **Zinc:** EFSA's tolerable upper level is **25 mg a day** for adults, food and supplements combined. Germany's BfR recommends no more than **6.5 mg** per daily dose in a supplement. Treatment trials often use higher doses for a few days; don't continue them long term without medical advice, as excess zinc can cause copper deficiency.
- **Vitamin C:** EFSA has not set an upper level for lack of data, but gram doses can upset the stomach. People prone to kidney stones should check with a doctor first.

## What makes sense this winter

1. **Diet first:** one orange or kiwi supplies a good share of daily vitamin C.
2. **Extreme physical effort** (marathons, mountaineering in the cold) is where vitamin C has the strongest evidence.
3. **If you want to try zinc**, start at the first symptoms, for a few days only, without exceeding long-term limits.
4. **Check your vitamin D:** low winter levels are common. See [vitamin D in a European winter](/en/guides/vitamin-d-winter-europe/).
5. **See a doctor** if you have a high fever, difficulty breathing or a cold lasting more than 10 days.
