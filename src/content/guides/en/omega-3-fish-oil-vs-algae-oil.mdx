## The short answer

The NHS advises **at least 2 portions of fish a week, 1 of them oily** (salmon, mackerel, sardines, herring, trout). If you eat that, you probably already reach the reference intake for omega-3. If not, a supplement with **250–450 mg of EPA + DHA a day** matches what EFSA, the authorised health claims and UK dietitians use. **Fish oil** usually gives more EPA per pound or euro; **algae oil** is the vegan option and raises blood DHA comparably. Choose by **mg of EPA + DHA per dose**, not capsule size.

## What omega-3 may claim in the UK and EU

The Great Britain register has the same omega-3 claims as the EU register:

| Authorised claim | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| EPA and DHA contribute to the normal function of the heart | 250 mg EPA + DHA a day |
| DHA contributes to the maintenance of normal brain function | 250 mg DHA a day |
| DHA contributes to the maintenance of normal vision | 250 mg DHA a day |

There are no authorised claims for memory, mood, joints or preventing heart disease. In the UK, NICE tells doctors not to offer omega-3 fatty acid compounds to prevent cardiovascular disease and to tell people there is no evidence that they help (the prescription drug icosapent ethyl is a narrow exception), and the British Dietetic Association says supplements are not recommended for the general population because the evidence of benefit is inconclusive. See our [table of approved health claims](/en/guides/eu-approved-health-claims-supplements/).

## Fish oil, fish liver oil and pregnancy

* **Fish oil** comes from the body of oily fish and supplies EPA and DHA.
* **Fish liver oil** (cod liver oil) also contains a lot of **vitamin A**. The NHS says not to have more than 1.5 mg of vitamin A a day from food and supplements combined, and to avoid fish liver oil if you are pregnant or trying for a baby.
* **If you eat oily fish:** girls and women who may have a baby one day should have no more than 2 portions of oily fish a week, because of pollutants. Supplements are refined and are not counted in that limit, but check with your midwife or GP before taking one in pregnancy.

## Fish oil vs algae oil

| | Fish oil | Algae oil |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | Oily fish (anchovy, sardine, mackerel), cod liver | Cultivated microalgae |
| EPA and DHA | Both, often more EPA than DHA | Mostly DHA; some products add EPA |
| Vegan | No | Yes |
| Contaminants | Refined; UK and EU law set limits for dioxins and PCBs in marine oils | Controlled cultivation, no sea exposure |
| Absorption | Benchmark | A 2013 trial found similar blood DHA levels |
| Price per gram EPA + DHA | Usually lower | Usually higher |

The DHA in fish actually comes from the algae they eat. In a 2013 trial, 31 adults took 600 mg of DHA a day for two weeks: algae and fish oil raised blood DHA to similar levels, including in vegetarians and vegans, who started lower.

## The label trick: "1,000 mg fish oil"

Many capsules show "1,000 mg" in large print, but that is the **total oil**. What counts is the milligrams of **EPA and DHA**, which may be 300 mg in a standard capsule or more than 600 mg in a concentrate. Look for the "of which EPA" and "of which DHA" lines per daily dose.

An example from our database: **Norsan Omega-3 Total** liquid, sold in Germany, provides **1,040 mg EPA and 600 mg DHA** per 8 ml dose, plus 20 µg vitamin D. That is 1,640 mg of EPA + DHA a day — more than six times the reference intake and well below EFSA's safe level.

## How much is too much

- **EFSA:** up to **5 g a day** of supplemental EPA + DHA raises no safety concerns for adults.
- **BfR (Germany, 2023):** omega-3 preparations may increase the risk of **atrial fibrillation** in people with existing or impending heart disease, with the highest risk at the highest dose studied, 4 g a day. It advises these people to take them only after consulting a doctor.

**Talk to your GP** before taking omega-3 if you have heart disease, take blood thinners or are due for surgery.

## How to compare prices

Calculate the **cost per gram of EPA + DHA**:

**pack price ÷ daily doses per pack ÷ g EPA + DHA per dose = cost per gram**

A cheap algae oil with little DHA per capsule can cost more per gram of omega-3 than a pricier fish-oil concentrate. Keep the pack closed, cool and away from light, and throw away capsules that smell rancid.
