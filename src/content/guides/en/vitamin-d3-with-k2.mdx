## The short answer

You don't need K2 for vitamin D to work. European authorities do not recommend combining them, and the EU has authorised no health claim for the combination. A small dose of K2 (up to **25 µg a day**) does not appear harmful for most people, but it has **no proven benefit**. What does matter: if you take **blood thinners such as warfarin**, don't add K2 without your doctor, and avoid **mega-doses of vitamin D** taken every few days.

## What official bodies say

| Source | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| BfR (Germany, 2024 and 2025) | Recommended maximum of **25 µg K2** and **20 µg vitamin D** per daily dose in supplements. High-dose D + K2 combinations have hardly been studied |
| BfR (2025) | High vitamin D doses taken every few days or weeks raise the risk of dosing errors and are linked to more falls and fractures in some studies. A low daily dose is preferable |
| Verbraucherzentrale (Germany, 2026) | Vitamin D alone is enough. Adding K2 is harmless but has no proven benefit |
| German osteoporosis guideline (2023) | K2 should not be used as a specific treatment for osteoporosis |
| EFSA | Adequate intake for vitamin K: **70 µg a day** for adults, normally covered by food |

## What EU law allows

Vitamin K has two authorised claims: it contributes to normal **blood clotting** and to the maintenance of normal **bones**. Vitamin D has its own (immune system, bones, muscles, calcium absorption). There are **none** for the combination, for "directing calcium to the bones" or for artery health. See our [table of EU-approved health claims](/en/guides/eu-approved-health-claims-supplements/).

## What about arteries?

The sales argument is that K2 activates a protein that stops calcium being deposited in arteries. The evidence is preliminary: a 2023 meta-analysis of 14 trials found slightly slower coronary calcification in **4 trials with 424 people**, mostly with kidney disease or other conditions. Results for other arteries were mostly negative, and the authors call for better-designed trials. There is no evidence that healthy people taking normal vitamin D doses need K2.

## Careful with blood thinners

According to the BfR, K2 is about **3.5 times as potent as K1** at weakening vitamin K antagonist drugs such as **warfarin, phenprocoumon (Marcumar) or acenocoumarol (Sintrom)**. Sudden changes in vitamin K intake are a serious risk for these patients, which is why the BfR recommends a warning on supplements containing vitamin K. **If you take one of these drugs, talk to your doctor before taking K2.**

## How to choose

1. **Vitamin D alone** is enough for most people. Our guide to [vitamin D in a European winter](/en/guides/vitamin-d-winter-europe/) explains how much to take.
2. **If you prefer D3 + K2**, pick a product with **25 µg of K2 or less** per daily dose.
3. **Small daily doses** beat weekly or monthly mega-doses, unless prescribed.
4. **No K2 on blood thinners** without medical advice.
