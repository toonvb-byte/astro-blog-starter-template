faq:
  - q: Which supplements should not be taken together?
    a: >-
      The main ones: iron with calcium, tea, coffee or dairy (leave about 2
      hours), iron with zinc (take them at different times), and high-dose zinc
      for a long time, which lowers copper. With medicines: magnesium, zinc and
      iron with tetracycline or quinolone antibiotics, magnesium with
      bisphosphonates, and calcium, iron or magnesium with levothyroxine.
  - q: Can I take iron and vitamin C together?
    a: >-
      Yes, and you should. "Vitamin C increases iron absorption" is an
      authorised EU health claim. A glass of orange juice with your iron tablet
      or with a plant-based meal does the job.
  - q: Can I take vitamin D and magnesium together?
    a: >-
      Yes. There is no known problem taking them at the same time. Take vitamin
      D with a meal that contains some fat, and keep magnesium from supplements
      at or below 250 mg a day.
  - q: Can I take zinc and magnesium together?
    a: >-
      At normal doses, yes. The interaction to watch with zinc is copper: more
      than 25 mg of zinc a day for weeks can lower copper. And keep zinc and
      iron at different times of day.
  - q: How long should I wait between levothyroxine and supplements?
    a: >-
      UK and US guidance: at least 4 hours between levothyroxine and calcium,
      iron or magnesium, including multivitamins that contain them. Most people
      take levothyroxine on waking and supplements with lunch or dinner. Ask
      your pharmacist about anything else you take.
  - q: Is it better to take supplements in the morning or at night?
    a: >-
      For most supplements the time of day matters less than taking them
      regularly. The exceptions: melatonin shortly before bed (that is the
      condition of its EU claim), iron on an empty stomach if you tolerate it,
      and vitamin D with a meal.

## The short answer

You don't need a spreadsheet for most supplements. Three rules cover nearly everything: take **iron with vitamin C and away from calcium, dairy, tea and coffee**; keep **minerals (magnesium, zinc, calcium, iron) away from some antibiotics, osteoporosis tablets and thyroid tablets**; and don't take **high-dose zinc for weeks**. The tables below give the gaps the official sources use. If you take a prescription medicine, your pharmacist can check your exact list in a minute.

## Supplement with supplement, food or drink

| Combination | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron + vitamin C** | Vitamin C increases iron absorption. This is an authorised EU health claim | Take them **together**, for example iron with a glass of orange juice |
| **Iron + tea, coffee, milk, eggs** | They reduce how much iron gets into your body | Leave about **2 hours** after the iron (NHS) |
| **Iron + calcium** (also magnesium and antacids) | Calcium reduces the effect of iron | Keep them **2 hours apart** (iron medicine leaflets) |
| **Iron + zinc** | Iron can stop zinc working as well as it should | Take them at **different times of day** |
| **High-dose zinc, for weeks** | Zinc blocks copper absorption and can cause copper deficiency | Stay at **25 mg of zinc a day or less** from supplements unless a doctor advises more |
| **Vitamin D + a meal with some fat** | In one trial, vitamin D taken with a meal containing fat was absorbed about **32 % better** than with a fat-free meal | Take it with your **main meal** |
| **Calcium carbonate + food** | Carbonate is absorbed best with food; citrate is absorbed well with or without | Take **carbonate with a meal** |

## Supplement with medicines

| Combination | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Magnesium or zinc + tetracycline or quinolone antibiotics** (for example doxycycline, ciprofloxacin) | The mineral binds the antibiotic, which then works less well | Take the antibiotic **at least 2 hours before or 4 to 6 hours after** the supplement (NIH) |
| **Iron + the same antibiotics** | Same problem | Keep them **at least 2 hours apart** (iron medicine leaflets) |
| **Magnesium + bisphosphonates** (for example alendronate) | Less of the osteoporosis medicine is absorbed | Keep them **at least 2 hours apart** (NIH) |
| **Calcium, iron or magnesium + levothyroxine** | Less thyroid hormone is absorbed | Leave **at least 4 hours**; a multivitamin with these minerals counts too (NHS, Mayo Clinic) |
| **Vitamin K2 + warfarin-type blood thinners** (vitamin K antagonists) | K2 weakens these medicines, and sudden changes in vitamin K intake are risky | Don't start or stop K2 **without your doctor or anticoagulation clinic** |

This is not a complete interaction list. Proton pump inhibitors and some diuretics, for example, can change your magnesium levels over time. **Tell your pharmacist every supplement you take**, including multivitamins.

## When in the day

| Supplement | Best timing | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron** | On an empty stomach (30 minutes before or 2 hours after eating) if you tolerate it; with food if it upsets your stomach | NHS. In iron-depleted women, a single morning dose on alternate days was absorbed better than daily split doses |
| **Vitamin D** | With a meal that contains some fat | Better absorption |
| **Magnesium** | Split over the day, at least two doses | The BfR advises this for tolerance |
| **Melatonin** | Shortly before bed | The EU claim (falling asleep faster) applies to **1 mg taken close to bedtime** |
| **Creatine** | Any time, every day | The EU claim is for **3 g a day**; it doesn't depend on timing |
| **Calcium** | Carbonate with a meal, ideally 500 mg or less at a time | The NIH notes absorption is best at doses of 500 mg or less |

## A practical day

If you take several supplements and a medicine, a simple split solves most of the table:

1. **On waking:** levothyroxine (if prescribed), with water only.
2. **Mid-morning, 2 hours away from coffee:** iron with a glass of orange juice, if you take iron.
3. **Lunch or dinner:** vitamin D, omega-3 and a multivitamin with food.
4. **Evening:** magnesium (or half your dose), zinc if you take it on its own.

Check the totals too. A multivitamin plus a separate zinc, magnesium or vitamin D product can push you over the safe upper levels. Our [maximum levels by country](/en/guides/supplement-maximum-levels-europe/) show the limits, and [how to read a supplement label](/en/guides/how-to-read-eu-supplement-label/) shows where to find the real amount per daily dose. For iron specifically, read [iron and tiredness](/en/guides/iron-supplements-tiredness/) first: iron only helps if you are low in it.
