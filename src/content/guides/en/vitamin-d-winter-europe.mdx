## The short answer

Most vitamin D comes from your skin when it is exposed to sunlight, not from food. In winter the sun is lower, people spend more time indoors and levels fall, in southern Europe too. If you are in a higher-risk group, ask your doctor for a **25(OH)D blood test**. If you decide to supplement without a test, **10–25 µg (400–1,000 IU) of vitamin D3 a day** sits within what European bodies consider and well below the safe upper level.

## How low do levels get in Europe?

Two official or peer-reviewed data points, from the two markets we cover:

| Country | Data | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Germany | National health survey of adults (DEGS1, Robert Koch Institute) | 61.6% below 50 nmol/l; 30.2% below 30 nmol/l |
| Spain | 206 healthy blood donors in Burgos and Valencia (2022) | Average levels 18–27% lower after winter than after summer |

EFSA set its adequate intake so that most people reach **50 nmol/l (20 ng/ml)**. In the Spanish study, the post-winter average in Burgos was 17.7 ng/ml, below that mark, and the authors concluded that levels were lower than expected for a healthy population. In Germany, levels were clearly lower from November to April than in summer.

## Who is at higher risk

- Older people, because the skin makes less vitamin D with age.
- People who spend little time outdoors or work indoors.
- People with darker skin, who need more sunlight to make the same amount.
- People who cover most of their skin or always use high-factor sunscreen.
- People living with obesity, gut conditions that affect absorption, or on certain medicines.

## How much, and the safe maximum

| Authority | Figure for adults | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EFSA (EU) | 15 µg (600 IU) a day | Adequate intake, assuming little skin production |
| EFSA (EU), reconfirmed 2023 | 100 µg (4,000 IU) a day | Tolerable upper level, food and supplements combined |
| BfR (Germany) | 20 µg (800 IU) per daily dose | Recommended maximum in a supplement |
| Spain | No national maximum | EU rules apply |

Taking more than 100 µg a day for long periods can raise blood calcium. Doses above that belong under medical supervision. Our guide to [supplement maximum levels in Europe](/en/guides/supplement-maximum-levels-europe/) compares vitamin D limits across 13 markets.

## What vitamin D can claim in the EU

EU-authorised claims say vitamin D contributes to:

- the normal **function of the immune system**;
- the maintenance of normal **bones** and teeth;
- the maintenance of normal **muscle function**;
- normal absorption and use of **calcium and phosphorus**.

There is no authorised claim for mood, winter blues, tiredness or preventing colds.

## D3 or D2, drops or capsules

- **D3 vs D2:** a 2012 meta-analysis found that **D3 raises blood levels** more effectively than D2. Most supplements already use D3.
- **Drops vs capsules:** both work; pick the one you will take every day. Drops are usually cheaper per dose. Check the µg **per drop**, not per millilitre.
- **With K2:** there is no authorised claim for the combination, and vitamin D does not need K2 to work.
