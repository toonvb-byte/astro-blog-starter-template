## The short answer

Most supplement packs in Europe can only say a small number of things, and only in set words. **Vitamins and minerals** have dozens of authorised claims. A few other ingredients — **omega-3 DHA and EPA, creatine, melatonin, glucomannan** — have one or two each. **Plants and extracts** (ashwagandha, turmeric, valerian, rhodiola, milk thistle) have **none**, and nor do glucosamine, probiotics or collagen on their own.

When a pack promises more than the claims in the table below, it is going beyond what EU law allows.

## How the rules work

- **The register is the list.** Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006 says a health claim may only be used if it is authorised and listed in the EU register. Most general claims are in Regulation (EU) No 432/2012.
- **The wording is fixed.** Brands may adapt the phrasing slightly for consumers, but the meaning must stay the same. "Contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue" cannot become "beats fatigue".
- **There is a minimum dose.** For vitamins and minerals, the product must be at least a "source" of the nutrient: **15% of the nutrient reference value** per daily dose. Other ingredients have their own conditions (3 g of creatine, 1 mg of melatonin, 250 mg of DHA).
- **No disease claims.** A supplement may not claim to prevent, treat or cure a disease.

## The table: what each ingredient may claim

Claims are summarised; the exact wording is in the register. Minimum = amount per recommended daily dose needed to use the claims.

| Ingredient | May claim that it contributes to… | Minimum per daily dose | Common claims that are **not** authorised |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Magnesium | Reduction of tiredness and fatigue; normal muscle function; nervous system; psychological function; energy metabolism; bones and teeth; electrolyte balance | 56 mg (15% of 375 mg) | Better sleep, less anxiety, fewer cramps, migraine |
| Vitamin D | Normal immune function; bones; teeth; muscle function; absorption of calcium and phosphorus; normal blood calcium | 0.75 µg (15% of 5 µg) | Mood, winter blues, preventing colds, energy |
| Vitamin C | Immune system; collagen formation for skin, bones, cartilage, gums, teeth and blood vessels; reduction of tiredness and fatigue; iron absorption; protection of cells from oxidative stress | 12 mg (15% of 80 mg) | Preventing or curing colds |
| Zinc | Immune system; normal skin, hair, nails and bones; cognitive function; fertility and reproduction; normal testosterone levels in the blood; vision | 1.5 mg (15% of 10 mg) | Testosterone "booster", hair growth |
| Iron | Reduction of tiredness and fatigue; red blood cell and haemoglobin formation; oxygen transport; cognitive function; immune system | 2.1 mg (15% of 14 mg) | Energy for people who are not iron-deficient |
| Vitamin B12 | Reduction of tiredness and fatigue; energy metabolism; nervous system; psychological function; red blood cell formation; immune system | 0.375 µg (15% of 2.5 µg) | "Energy shot", weight loss |
| Vitamin B6 | Reduction of tiredness and fatigue; regulation of hormonal activity; nervous system; psychological function; red blood cell formation; immune system | 0.21 mg (15% of 1.4 mg) | PMS relief, mood |
| Folate / folic acid | Maternal tissue growth in pregnancy; blood formation; psychological function; immune system; reduction of tiredness and fatigue | 30 µg (15% of 200 µg) | — (plus one disease-risk claim on neural tube defects, see below) |
| Calcium | Maintenance of normal bones and teeth; muscle function; blood clotting; neurotransmission | 120 mg (15% of 800 mg) | Preventing osteoporosis |
| Vitamin K | Normal blood clotting; maintenance of normal bones | 11.25 µg (15% of 75 µg) | Arterial health, "directs calcium" |
| Selenium | Immune system; normal thyroid function; normal hair and nails; spermatogenesis; protection of cells from oxidative stress | 8.25 µg (15% of 55 µg) | Cancer prevention |
| Iodine | Normal thyroid function and thyroid hormones; cognitive function; nervous system; normal skin | 22.5 µg (15% of 150 µg) | Weight loss, metabolism "boost" |
| Omega-3 DHA | Maintenance of normal brain function; maintenance of normal vision | 250 mg DHA | Memory improvement, ADHD |
| Omega-3 EPA + DHA | Normal function of the heart | 250 mg EPA + DHA | Lowering cholesterol (without the specific conditions), preventing heart disease |
| Creatine | Increases physical performance in successive bursts of short-term, high-intensity exercise | 3 g creatine | Muscle growth, fat loss, brain health |

| Melatonin | Reduction of time taken to fall asleep; alleviation of jet lag | 1 mg close to bedtime (sleep); 0.5 mg on travel days (jet lag) | Deeper sleep, anxiety, anti-ageing |
| Glucomannan | Weight loss in the context of an energy-restricted diet | 3 g a day in 3 doses of 1 g, with water, before meals | Weight loss without a diet, appetite control |

Two notes on the vitamins and minerals: the claim lists above are the main ones, not every authorised claim; and the minimum shown is the "source" threshold most supplements use. ## Ingredients with no authorised claims

| Ingredient | Why there is no claim |
| --- | --- |
| Ashwagandha, turmeric/curcumin, valerian, rhodiola, milk thistle, ginseng and other botanicals | Claims for plants have been "on hold" since 2010 and never authorised |
| Glucosamine and chondroitin | Joint claims assessed by EFSA and rejected |
| Probiotics | All claims for specific strains assessed so far have been rejected; several countries treat the word "probiotic" itself as a health claim |
| Collagen | No authorised claim of its own; products rely on added vitamin C |
| Caffeine in supplements | No claim authorised for food supplements |

This is why so many collagen, joint and "immune" products contain a vitamin or mineral: it is the only way to make a legal claim on the pack. See our [ashwagandha guide](/en/guides/ashwagandha-eu-legal-status/) for an example of a botanical with no claim and growing regulatory scrutiny.

## Disease risk reduction claims

A small number of claims may mention reducing a disease **risk factor**, under strict conditions. The best-known one for supplements is **folic acid**: supplemental folic acid increases maternal folate status, and low maternal folate is a risk factor for neural tube defects in the developing foetus. The claim applies to products providing at least 400 µg of folic acid a day, taken from at least one month before conception until three months after.

## How to use this table when you shop

1. **Read the claim, then the dose.** A "tiredness and fatigue" claim on a magnesium product is only valid if the daily dose contains at least 56 mg of magnesium.
2. **Spot the borrowed claim.** If a collagen, ashwagandha or turmeric product carries a claim, check which added vitamin or mineral it comes from.
3. **Treat anything else as marketing.** Phrases like "supports sleep", "detox", "boosts testosterone" or "for healthy joints" with no matching nutrient have no authorised basis.
