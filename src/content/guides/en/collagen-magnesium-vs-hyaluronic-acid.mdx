## The short answer

Collagen with magnesium and collagen with hyaluronic acid are best-sellers, especially in Spain, but **neither headline ingredient has an EU-authorised health claim**. What the pack may legally promise comes from the **magnesium** (bones, muscles, tiredness) or from **vitamin C** when it is added (collagen formation). The evidence for collagen on skin is disputed, and for oral hyaluronic acid it is thin. If you buy these, you are mainly paying for the magnesium and taking a bet on the rest.

## What each product may claim

| Product                       | Ingredient with an authorised claim         | What the pack may say                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Collagen with magnesium       | Magnesium                                   | Contributes to the maintenance of normal bones, normal muscle function and the reduction of tiredness and fatigue |
| Collagen with hyaluronic acid | None, unless vitamins or minerals are added | Nothing about skin or joints, except what added vitamin C or another nutrient allows                              |
| Collagen with vitamin C       | Vitamin C                                   | Contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of skin, cartilage and bones                     |

EFSA assessed a joint-health claim for collagen hydrolysate and found that a cause-and-effect relationship had not been established; the EU refused it in 2012. That is why so many collagen products add magnesium or vitamin C. See our [table of EU-approved health claims](/en/guides/eu-approved-health-claims-supplements/).

## What the evidence shows

| Ingredient                  | Key study                                         | Result                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Collagen (skin)             | 2025 meta-analysis, 23 trials, 1,474 participants | Pooled results suggested better hydration and elasticity, but **only in industry-funded studies**. Independent and higher-quality trials found no effect. Industry groups dispute the analysis |
| Oral hyaluronic acid (skin) | 2025 meta-analysis, 7 trials                      | Improvements in hydration, elasticity and wrinkle depth, in **few, small and varied** studies                                                                                                  |
| Collagen (joints)           | Trials with 5–10 g a day                          | Mixed results; possible small benefit                                                                                                                                                          |

The honest conclusion: collagen for skin is currently a **disputed bet**. Critics of the meta-analysis have a point — "collagen" covers very different ingredients and doses — but when only manufacturer-funded studies come out positive, caution is warranted.

## The detail almost nobody checks: the dose

Many tablet products contain **only a few grams of collagen** per daily dose, while studies typically use **2.5–10 g**. An example from our database: Ana María Lajusticia *Colágeno con magnesio* tablets list **6 tablets a day** for **3.6 g of collagen and 185 mg of magnesium**.

Two practical consequences:

1. **Take fewer tablets than listed** and you get very little collagen.
2. **Take another magnesium supplement too** and you need to add them up. EFSA sets the upper level for magnesium from supplements at **250 mg a day**; 185 mg from this product leaves only 65 mg of headroom. Above that, diarrhoea becomes more likely.

## So which makes sense?

| If what you want is…                 | Most sensible choice                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Magnesium, with collagen as an extra | Collagen with magnesium, counting your total daily magnesium                                    |
| Better-hydrated skin                 | Neither has an authorised claim. Oral hyaluronic acid has some evidence; collagen's is disputed |
| Joint support                        | Weak evidence for collagen. See a doctor if pain persists                                       |
| Best value                           | Compare **cost per gram of collagen** and per mg of magnesium, not the tub price                |

Our guides to [magnesium forms](/en/guides/magnesium-forms-compared/) and [magnesium for sleep](/en/guides/magnesium-for-sleep/) explain what magnesium does and how much to take.

**Talk to a doctor** before taking magnesium if you have kidney disease, or take antibiotics, bisphosphonates or proton pump inhibitors long term.
