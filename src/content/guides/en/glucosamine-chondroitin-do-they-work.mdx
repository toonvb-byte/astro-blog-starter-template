## The short answer

Glucosamine and chondroitin may ease knee osteoarthritis pain for some people, but the evidence is inconsistent and experts disagree. If you want to try them, use **trial-level doses** of the forms with the best data, give it **about six months**, and stop if nothing changes. They are not for acute injuries, and swollen or hot joints need a doctor.

## The key trials

| Trial | Who | Daily doses | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GAIT (USA, 2006) | 1,583 people with knee osteoarthritis, 24 weeks | Glucosamine 1,500 mg, chondroitin sulphate 1,200 mg, alone or together | No significant benefit over placebo overall. In the small group with moderate to severe pain, 79% responded to the combination vs 54% to placebo |
| MOVES (France, Germany, Poland, Spain, 2016) | 606 people with painful knee osteoarthritis, 6 months | Chondroitin sulphate 1,200 mg + glucosamine hydrochloride 1,500 mg | Pain fell by 50% with the combination and 50% with celecoxib: equally effective |

Both results come with caveats. In GAIT, the anti-inflammatory celecoxib only beat placebo by about ten points (70% vs 60% responders), which shows how strong the placebo effect is in joint pain. The moderate-to-severe subgroup was small, so the researchers called the finding preliminary. MOVES compared the supplement with a drug rather than with placebo, and it was funded by Bioibérica, the Barcelona-based company that supplied the product.

## Why the guidelines disagree

In 2019, two expert bodies published osteoarthritis guidelines that reach opposite conclusions from much the same studies:

- **ESCEO** (European Society for Clinical and Economic Aspects of Osteoporosis, Osteoarthritis and Musculoskeletal Diseases) recommends **pharmaceutical-grade (crystalline) glucosamine sulphate and chondroitin sulphate** as background therapy for knee osteoarthritis, before topical anti-inflammatory gels.
- **OARSI** (Osteoarthritis Research Society International) **strongly recommends against** all glucosamine and chondroitin formulations, including pharmaceutical grade.

A 2020 comparison of the two, written by authors from both groups, put the difference down to how each judged the quality and risk of bias of the same evidence. In practice: the best case for these ingredients rests on **specific pharmaceutical-grade products**, not on any supplement that lists them.

## Supplement or medicine?

In Spain, glucosamine sulphate and chondroitin sulphate are also sold as **medicines** in pharmacies, made to pharmaceutical standards. That is the type of product ESCEO's recommendation refers to. Food supplements can use other forms, such as glucosamine hydrochloride, and vary more in quality. If you want to follow ESCEO, ask your pharmacist about the medicine versions.

The EU has **no authorised health claim** for glucosamine, chondroitin or MSM. EFSA assessed glucosamine's joint claims in 2009 and 2012 and found no proven cause-and-effect link. That is why many joint supplements add vitamin C, which may claim that it contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of cartilage.

## Who should be careful

- **Shellfish allergy:** most glucosamine is made from crustacean shells. Choose a product made by fermentation if you are allergic.
- **Warfarin and other blood thinners:** glucosamine may increase the risk of bruising or bleeding. Talk to your doctor first.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** there is little safety data, so avoid it unless your doctor agrees.

## How to try it sensibly

1. Get a diagnosis first. Knee pain has many causes, and osteoarthritis is only one.
2. Choose **glucosamine sulphate** at about 1,500 mg a day, with or without chondroitin sulphate at 800–1,200 mg, or ask for the pharmacy medicine.
3. Note your pain and stiffness at the start.
4. Reassess after **about six months**, and stop if there is no clear difference.
