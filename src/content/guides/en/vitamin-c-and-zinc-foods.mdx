## The short answer

Vitamin C is the easiest nutrient to cover with food: half a raw red pepper, an orange or a serving of broccoli is around the NRV. Zinc takes more attention if you eat little meat or shellfish, because phytates in grains and pulses reduce how much you absorb. Neither, on the plate or in a pill, prevents colds: we explain the evidence in [zinc and vitamin C for colds](/en/guides/zinc-vitamin-c-for-colds/).

## How much you need

| Nutrient | EFSA reference intake | NRV on labels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vitamin C | 110 mg/day (men), 95 mg/day (women) | 80 mg |
| Zinc | 9.4–16.3 mg/day (men), 7.5–12.7 mg/day (women) | 10 mg |

The zinc range depends on the phytate in your diet: the low figure assumes about 300 mg of phytate a day, the high one about 1,200 mg, typical of diets with lots of whole grains and pulses.

## Vitamin C: how much each serving gives you

Sorted by vitamin C **per serving**. The last column shows whether the food meets the EU threshold to be advertised as a "source of" vitamin C (15% of the NRV per 100 g) or "high in" vitamin C (30%).

| Food | Serving | Vitamin C (mg) | % NRV | EU threshold (per 100 g) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orange juice, freshly squeezed | 1 glass (200 ml) | 100 | 125% | high |
| Broccoli, cooked | 150 g | 97 | 122% | high |
| Red pepper, raw | half (75 g) | 96 | 120% | high |
| Brussels sprouts, cooked | 150 g | 93 | 116% | high |
| Strawberries | 150 g | 88 | 110% | high |
| Orange | 1 medium (150 g) | 80 | 100% | high |
| Kiwi | 1 (75 g) | 70 | 87% | high |
| Cauliflower, cooked | 150 g | 66 | 83% | high |
| Green pepper, raw | half (75 g) | 60 | 75% | high |
| Red cabbage, raw | 75 g | 43 | 53% | high |
| Mandarins | 2 (150 g) | 40 | 50% | high |
| Sauerkraut | 150 g | 22 | 28% | source |
| Tomato | 1 medium (120 g) | 16 | 21% | source |
| Potatoes, boiled | 200 g | 15 | 18% | — |
| Parsley, fresh | 1 tbsp (5 g) | 7 | 8% | high |

**What the table shows:**

- **Almost every winter vegetable** is "high in" vitamin C: broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cauliflower, red cabbage and peppers.
- **Heat costs you.** According to the NIH, vitamin C is destroyed by heat and long storage; steaming and microwaving keep more than boiling. That is why 75 g of raw red pepper gives so much.
- **Citrus isn't the only option.** A serving of cooked broccoli gives more than an orange.
- **Sauerkraut** is a fair source, not an outstanding one: 22 mg per 150 g.

## Zinc: how much each serving gives you

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oysters | 6 (80 g) | 13.3 | 133% | high |
| Beef mince, cooked | 125 g | 7.9 | 79% | high |
| Lean beef steak, cooked | 125 g | 7.2 | 72% | high |
| Pork tenderloin, cooked | 125 g | 3.0 | 30% | source |
| Mussels, cooked (meat) | 100 g | 2.7 | 27% | source |
| Chicken thigh, cooked | 125 g | 2.4 | 24% | source |
| Pumpkin seeds, hulled | a handful (30 g) | 2.3 | 23% | high |
| Chickpeas, cooked | 150 g | 2.3 | 23% | source |
| Lentils, cooked | 150 g | 1.9 | 19% | — |
| Rolled oats | 50 g | 1.8 | 18% | high |
| Cashews | a handful (30 g) | 1.7 | 17% | high |
| Prawns, cooked | 100 g | 1.6 | 16% | source |
| Emmental | 30 g | 1.3 | 13% | high |
| Manchego cheese | 30 g | 1.2 | 12% | high |
| Wholemeal bread | 2 slices (60 g) | 1.1 | 11% | source |
| Egg | 1 medium (60 g) | 0.8 | 8% | — |

**What the table shows:**

- **Oysters** are by far the richest source of zinc; the NIH puts them first too.
- **Red meat** is the everyday source: a 125 g beef steak gives 72% of the NRV.
- **Plant foods** give less, and the NIH notes that phytates in grains and pulses bind zinc and reduce its absorption. That is why EFSA sets a higher need for diets rich in phytates.
- **Hard cheese** helps: 30 g of Emmental or Manchego gives over 1 mg.

## Two recipes with the numbers

One comes from our Spanish edition, one from our German edition.

### Orange, red pepper and pumpkin seed salad (2 servings)

**Ingredients:** 2 oranges (300 g peeled), 1 red pepper (150 g), 20 g roasted pumpkin seeds, half a spring onion (50 g), 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil, salt and pepper.

1. Peel the oranges with a knife, removing the pith, and slice them.
2. Cut the pepper into thin strips and the spring onion into fine slices.
3. Toss, dress with oil, salt and pepper, and finish with the seeds.

**Per serving:** about **178 mg vitamin C** (222% of the NRV) and 1.1 mg zinc. The orange and pepper are raw, so no vitamin C is lost to cooking.

### Brussels sprouts and lentil pan with pumpkin seeds (2 servings)

**Ingredients:** 300 g Brussels sprouts, 250 g cooked lentils (such as green or Puy lentils), 20 g pumpkin seeds, 1 onion (100 g), 2 tbsp rapeseed oil, 1 tsp mustard, salt and pepper.

1. Halve the sprouts and steam them for 5–6 minutes.
2. Fry the onion in the oil, add the sprouts and lentils and fry for 3 more minutes.
3. Season with mustard, salt and pepper and scatter over the pumpkin seeds.

**Per serving:** about **99 mg vitamin C** (124% of the NRV), 2.9 mg zinc (29%) and 6.9 mg iron (49%).

## When food isn't enough

- **If you are vegan** and eat lots of whole grains and pulses, keep an eye on zinc. According to the NIH, soaking beans, grains and seeds in water for several hours before cooking may help you absorb more.
- **If you're considering a zinc supplement**, don't take more than you need: EFSA sets the safe upper limit at 25 mg a day in total, and the NIH warns that 50 mg or more over weeks can reduce copper absorption. ## How we calculated the numbers

Values per 100 g come from the USDA's FoodData Central database (SR Legacy), checked against Spain's BEDCA database; Manchego comes from BEDCA. Vegetables marked "cooked" use the value for boiled vegetables. NutriMetrics calculates the servings, percentages and recipe values. The % NRV uses the references in Regulation (EU) 1169/2011: 80 mg of vitamin C and 10 mg of zinc. Real amounts vary with the variety, storage and cooking method.
