## Melatonin formats compared

| Format | Typical dose | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tablets, immediate release | 1–1.9 mg | Matches the EU claim for falling asleep |
| Prolonged release ("retard") | 1–1.98 mg | Spreads release over the night; mainly studied in older adults |
| Gummies | 1–1.9 mg | Look like sweets; keep away from children |
| Drops and sprays | 0.5–1.9 mg | Easy to take less; check mg per drop or spray |
| Sleep "complexes" | 1–1.95 mg plus plants or B6 | Only the melatonin has an authorised claim |

## What the evidence says

Melatonin is a hormone your body releases in the evening; a supplement mainly **shifts the timing** of sleep. A 2013 meta-analysis found people with primary sleep disorders fell asleep about 7 minutes sooner and slept about 8 minutes longer, which fits the modest EU claim. It is most useful for **jet lag** and a delayed body clock.

Rules differ sharply across Europe, from a food supplement below 2 mg in Spain to a prescription medicine in the UK. Our [melatonin guide](/en/guides/melatonin-dose-legal-status-europe/) has the full country table, and [magnesium for sleep](/en/guides/magnesium-for-sleep/) covers the most common alternative.
