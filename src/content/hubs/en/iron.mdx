## Iron forms compared

| Form | What it is | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ferrous sulphate | Classic iron salt, common in medicines | Cheap and effective; more stomach upset and constipation |
| Ferrous fumarate / gluconate | Other iron(II) salts | Similar to sulphate; gluconate gives less iron per mg |
| Iron bisglycinate | Iron bound to the amino acid glycine | Often better tolerated at lower doses |
| Liposomal / "sucrosomial" iron | Iron in a fat or sugar coating | Marketed as gentle; little independent evidence |
| Liquid tonics with plant extracts | Iron gluconate in a fruit and herb base | Low dose; check iron per daily serving |

## What the evidence says

Iron deficiency is common in women who menstruate, pregnant women, frequent blood donors and people on plant-based diets. In a Swiss trial of 198 tired women **without anaemia** but with low ferritin, iron reduced fatigue scores by 48%, against 29% with placebo. Without deficiency, extra iron brings no benefit, and iron that the body cannot get rid of accumulates.

Absorption is better when iron is taken in the morning, every other day, with vitamin C and away from coffee, tea and calcium, according to studies in iron-depleted women. Read [iron and tiredness](/en/guides/iron-supplements-tiredness/) for the ferritin levels and dosing in detail.
