## Vitamin C forms compared

| Form | What it is | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ascorbic acid | Plain vitamin C | Cheapest and well absorbed; can be acidic |
| Buffered (sodium or calcium ascorbate) | Vitamin C as a mineral salt | Gentler on the stomach; adds some sodium or calcium |
| Ester-C | Calcium ascorbate with vitamin C metabolites | Branded buffered form; little evidence of extra benefit |
| Liposomal | Vitamin C wrapped in fat particles | Expensive; thin independent evidence |
| With bioflavonoids or rosehip | Ascorbic acid plus plant extracts | The extracts add little vitamin C themselves |

## What the evidence says

Your body absorbs most of a moderate dose, but the share falls as the dose rises: according to the US National Institutes of Health, absorption drops to under 50% at intakes above about 1 g a day, and the excess leaves in urine. That is why very high-dose tablets offer little extra.

For **colds**, the Cochrane review of more than 10,000 people found that regular vitamin C did not reduce how many colds people caught, except under extreme physical stress such as marathons. It shortened colds slightly when taken daily. See [zinc and vitamin C for colds](/en/guides/zinc-vitamin-c-for-colds/).
