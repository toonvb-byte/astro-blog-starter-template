## CoQ10 products compared

| Type | What it is | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ubiquinone capsules | The standard oxidised form | Cheapest per mg; take with food |
| Ubiquinone softgels in oil | CoQ10 dissolved in oil | Usually better absorbed than dry powder |
| Ubiquinol | The reduced form | Marketed as better absorbed; costs more |
| Combinations with red yeast rice | CoQ10 plus monacolins | EU limits and warnings apply; not with statins |
| "Energy" blends with B vitamins | CoQ10 plus vitamins | Any legal claim comes from the B vitamins |

## What the evidence says

CoQ10 is part of how cells produce energy, and the body makes its own. Statins lower its level in the blood, which is why it is often suggested for statin muscle aches. According to the US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health, results across studies vary but the overall evidence does not support that use; CoQ10 probably has no meaningful effect on blood pressure, and research in heart failure is inconclusive.

That leaves CoQ10 as a supplement with a good safety record and weak evidence of benefit. If you take medicines, check our [guide to supplement labels](/en/guides/how-to-read-eu-supplement-label/) and talk to your pharmacist.
