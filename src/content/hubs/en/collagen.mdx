## Types of collagen supplement

| Type | What it is | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hydrolysed collagen (peptides) | Collagen broken into small peptides; bovine, porcine or fish | The form in most products and studies |
| Marine collagen | Hydrolysed collagen from fish skin or scales | No proven advantage; not for fish allergy |
| Branded peptides (e.g. Verisol, Peptan) | Specific hydrolysates used in some trials | Results apply to that ingredient and dose only |
| Undenatured type II (UC-II) | Small doses (around 40 mg) of native cartilage collagen | Marketed for joints; works differently from peptides |
| Gelatin | Partly broken-down collagen | Cheap; same building blocks |

## What the evidence says

For **skin**, a 2025 meta-analysis pooled 23 trials with 1,474 people. Overall results suggested better hydration and elasticity, but the benefit appeared **only in industry-funded trials**; independent and higher-quality studies found no effect. Manufacturers dispute that reading. For **joints**, trials at 5–10 g a day are mixed, with at most a small benefit.

That is why so many collagen products add magnesium, vitamin C or hyaluronic acid. Our guide on [collagen with magnesium or hyaluronic acid](/en/guides/collagen-magnesium-vs-hyaluronic-acid/) shows what each combination may legally claim and how to check the dose.
