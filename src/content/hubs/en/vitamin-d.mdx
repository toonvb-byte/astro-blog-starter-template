checklist:
  - >-
    Check the **dose per daily serving in µg**: 10 µg (400 IU) is the NHS
    amount, and 10–25 µg a day covers most adults in winter. Weekly or monthly mega-doses are not advised.
  - >-
    Pick **D3**. If you are vegan, look for D3 **from lichen**.
  - >-
    Take it **with a meal that contains some fat**, or choose drops or softgels
    in oil.
  - >-
    Count **vitamin D from other products**: multivitamins, fortified foods
    and omega-3 with added D all add up.
  - >-
    Only add **K2** if you want it for another reason, and never if you take a
    coumarin-type blood thinner such as warfarin.

faq:
  - q: How much vitamin D should I take a day?
    a: >-
      In the UK, the NHS advises 10 µg (400 IU) a day in autumn and winter,
      and all year for people at higher risk. Do not take more than 100 µg a
      day. EFSA's adequate intake is 15 µg a day. A 25(OH)D blood test is the only way to know if you are low.
  - q: Is vitamin D3 better than D2?
    a: >-
      Yes, for raising blood levels. A meta-analysis found D3 more effective
      than D2 at increasing serum 25(OH)D.
  - q: Do I need vitamin K2 with vitamin D?
    a: >-
      No. Vitamin D works without K2, the NHS advises plain vitamin D, and there
      is no authorised claim for the combination in the UK or the EU. K2 can interfere with some blood thinners.
  - q: Is 4,000 IU a day too much?
    a: >-
      4,000 IU (100 µg) is the most the NHS says adults should take a day, and
      EFSA's safe upper level for total intake. It is a ceiling, not a target:
      ten times the NHS daily amount, five times Germany's guidance for a single
      supplement and above the legal limit in several EU countries.

## Vitamin D forms compared

| Form | What it is | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D3 (cholecalciferol) | The form your skin makes; usually from lanolin (sheep's wool) | Raises blood levels more than D2 |
| Vegan D3 | D3 made from lichen | Same molecule, suitable for vegans |
| D2 (ergocalciferol) | Plant or fungal origin | Less effective at raising blood levels |
| Drops in oil | D3 dissolved in oil | Easy to adjust; check µg per drop |
| D3 + K2 | Combination with menaquinone (MK-7) | No authorised claim for the combination |

## What the evidence says

Your skin makes vitamin D from sunlight, but between roughly October and March the sun in the UK and most of Europe is too weak for that. In the UK national diet survey, **29% of adults** were below 25 nmol/l in January to March; in Germany's national health survey, **61.6% of adults** were below 50 nmol/l (a stricter threshold), and in Spain levels were clearly lower after winter than after summer. Older people, people with darker skin and people who rarely get sun are most at risk.

A daily supplement corrects low levels reliably. Whether it does much more than that in people who are not low is far less clear. Read [vitamin D in a European winter](/en/guides/vitamin-d-winter-europe/) for who needs it, and [D3 with K2](/en/guides/vitamin-d3-with-k2/) before paying extra for a combination.
