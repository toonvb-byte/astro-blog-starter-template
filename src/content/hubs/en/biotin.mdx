## Typical biotin products compared

| Type | Typical biotin per day | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plain biotin tablets | Often 5,000–10,000 µg | 100–200 times the reference intake |
| Biotin + zinc + selenium | Often 5,000–12,000 µg | Zinc and selenium have their own hair and nail claims; check the zinc dose |
| Gummies | Often 5,000–10,000 µg | Usually contain sugar; dosing less precise |
| B complex or multivitamin | Tens to a few hundred µg | Covers needs if you are not deficient |

## What the evidence says

Biotin is found in eggs, nuts, seeds, liver and many other foods, and severe deficiency has never been reported in healthy people eating a normal mixed diet, according to the US National Institutes of Health. The claims for hair, skin and nails in healthy people rest on case reports and a few small studies, mostly in children with specific conditions.

That makes biotin a weak first choice for thinning hair. Low iron is a more common, testable cause: see [iron and tiredness](/en/guides/iron-supplements-tiredness/). If you do buy biotin, a moderate dose carries the same EU claim and avoids lab-test problems.
