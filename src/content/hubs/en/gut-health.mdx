takeaways:
  - >-
    The **NHS** recommends **30 g of fibre a day** for adults; most people
    in the UK eat about 20 g. In the UK even the word **"probiotic"** on a
    label counts as a health claim, so many products say "live cultures"
    instead.
  - >-
    **Fibre supplements such as psyllium** are the first step for regular
    bowel movements; the 2023 US gastroenterology guideline suggests them for
    chronic constipation.
  - >-
    **Probiotics work strain by strain.** Some reduce diarrhoea linked to
    antibiotics; none has an authorised health claim.
  - >-
    **Lactase** is the one digestive enzyme with an authorised claim (UK and
    EU): it improves
    lactose digestion in people who have difficulty digesting lactose.
  - >-
    A lasting change in bowel habit, **blood in the stool or weight loss**
    needs a doctor, not a supplement.

checklist:
  - >-
    Start with **fibre from food** (wholegrains, legumes, fruit, vegetables);
    most adults eat less than 25 g a day.
  - >-
    For psyllium, **start low** (a teaspoon a day) with a **large glass of
    water**, and increase over one to two weeks.
  - >-
    For probiotics, choose a product that names the **strain** (for example
    *Lactobacillus rhamnosus* GG) and states the **CFU at the end of shelf
    life**, matched to your problem.
  - >-
    If dairy gives you trouble, **lactase tablets** taken with the meal have
    an authorised claim.
  - >-
    Avoid long-term use of **stimulant laxatives** sold as "detox" or
    "slimming" teas (senna, cascara).
cautions:
  - >-
    **Psyllium:** take it with plenty of water, and keep medicines about two
    hours apart, because fibre can reduce their absorption.
  - >-
    **Serious illness or weakened immune system:** don't take probiotics
    without medical advice.
  - >-
    **Red flags:** blood in the stool, unexplained weight loss, night-time
    symptoms or a change after age 50 need a medical check.

faq:
  - q: What is the best supplement for constipation?
    a: >-
      A soluble fibre supplement such as psyllium, taken with plenty of water,
      is the usual first step; the 2023 US gastroenterology guideline suggests
      fibre supplements for chronic constipation, on low-certainty evidence.
  - q: Do probiotics work?
    a: >-
      Some strains help with specific problems, such as reducing diarrhoea
      linked to antibiotics. Results don't carry over from one strain to
      another, and no probiotic has an authorised health claim.
  - q: Do lactase tablets work?
    a: >-
      Yes, for lactose intolerance: the UK and EU registers authorise the claim
      that lactase
      improves lactose digestion in people who have difficulty digesting it,
      at 4,500 FCC units with each lactose-containing meal.

## Which supplement for which digestive problem

| Problem | Option with the best support | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constipation | Psyllium or another fibre supplement | Increase slowly, with water |
| Loose stools or IBS | Psyllium (soluble fibre); some probiotic strains | Results vary by person and strain |
| Diarrhoea linked to antibiotics | Specific probiotics, started with the antibiotic | Check the strain on the label |
| Lactose intolerance | Lactase with the meal | authorised claim at 4,500 FCC |
| Bloating, heavy digestion | Artichoke extract (small trials) | Weak evidence; no authorised claim |
| "Detox" or "flat tummy" | Nothing with good evidence | Often stimulant laxatives; avoid long-term use |
