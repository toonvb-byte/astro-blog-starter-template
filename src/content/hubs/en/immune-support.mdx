checklist:
  - >-
    Look for the **vitamin or mineral behind the claim** on the back of the
    pack; herbs on the front don't carry an authorised claim.
  - >-
    In autumn and winter, a **daily vitamin D** supplement of 10 µg is the
    NHS advice for most people.
  - >-
    For a cold, **zinc lozenges** started within a day of the first symptoms
    are the option with the most support; stop after a few days.
  - >-
    **Add up** zinc and vitamin C from every product you take, including
    multivitamins.
  - >-
    Skip "immune shots" and gummies if the label shows small amounts of

cautions:
  - >-
    **Zinc:** more than 25 mg a day for a long time can cause copper
    deficiency. Don't keep taking high-dose "cold" products for weeks.
  - >-
    **Vitamin A:** avoid supplements containing vitamin A in pregnancy.
  - >-
    **Echinacea:** the European Medicines Agency's herbal monograph says not
    to use it with an allergy to the daisy family, and does not recommend it
    with autoimmune diseases, immune deficiencies or immunosuppression.
  - >-
    **Iron:** only take it if a blood test shows you need it.

faq:
  - q: What is the best supplement for the immune system?
    a: >-
      No supplement has been shown to boost a healthy immune system. If you are
      low in vitamin D in winter, a daily vitamin D supplement corrects that;
      the NHS advises 10 µg a day in autumn and winter. For colds, zinc
      lozenges started early may shorten them by about two days.
  - q: Which vitamins support the immune system?
    a: >-
      Vitamins A, B6, B12, C, D and folate have an authorised claim for the
      normal function of the immune system, as do the minerals copper, iron,
      selenium and zinc. A product needs 15% of the NRV of one of them per
      daily dose to use the claim.
  - q: Are immune complexes with echinacea worth it?
    a: >-
      Echinacea has no authorised claim, and a Cochrane review found only a
      weak, uncertain effect on colds. If a complex works at all, it is likely
      because of the vitamin C or zinc in it, which you can buy on their own.

## Which supplement for which winter question

| Question | Option with the best support | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not much sun between October and March | Vitamin D, 10 µg a day | Corrects a shortfall; no clear protection against colds overall |
| Cold just started | Zinc lozenges, started early | May shorten a cold by about 2 days; low certainty |
| Colds every winter | Daily vitamin C | Doesn't prevent them; shortens them by about 8% in adults |
| Hard physical training in the cold | Vitamin C | Fewer colds in trials of marathon runners and soldiers; an authorised claim at 200 mg extra a day |
| Want a "natural" option | Echinacea, elderberry, propolis | No authorised claim; weak or little evidence |
| Gut and immunity | Specific probiotic strains | May mean fewer respiratory infections; low certainty and strain-specific |
