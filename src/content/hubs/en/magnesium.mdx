faq:
  - q: Which form of magnesium is best?
    a: >-
      There is no single best form. Forms that dissolve well, such as citrate,
      are absorbed better than oxide; bisglycinate is usually gentle on the
      stomach but has little head-to-head data. For most people the dose of
      elemental magnesium and the price per 100 mg matter more than the form.
  - q: How much magnesium should I take a day?
    a: >-
      For healthy adults, EFSA considers up to 250 mg of magnesium a day from
      supplements safe, on top of what you eat. The EU reference intake of 375
      mg is for total intake from food and supplements together.
  - q: Does magnesium help you sleep?
    a: >-
      EU law does not allow that claim. In trials, older adults fell asleep
      about 17 minutes sooner, but the evidence is of low quality and the
      benefit seems larger in people who eat little magnesium.
  - q: Does magnesium prevent cramps?
    a: >-
      For older adults with ordinary muscle cramps, a Cochrane review found
      magnesium unlikely to make a meaningful difference. Cramps in pregnancy
      were less clear.

## Magnesium forms compared

The share of elemental magnesium differs a lot between compounds, so two products with the same "1,000 mg" on the front can deliver very different doses.

| Form | Elemental magnesium (approx.) | Compound needed for 200 mg magnesium | Usually chosen for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oxide | ~60% | ~330 mg | Lowest price per tablet |
| Carbonate | ~28% | ~710 mg | Cheap, often in effervescent tablets |
| Citrate | ~16% | ~1,250 mg | Good absorption at a mid price |
| Malate | ~15% | ~1,330 mg | Alternative to citrate |
| Bisglycinate | ~14% | ~1,430 mg | Gentle on the stomach |
| Chloride (hexahydrate) | ~12% | ~1,670 mg | Powders and liquids |
| L-threonate | ~7% | ~2,860 mg | Marketed for memory; little evidence |

Our [magnesium forms guide](/en/guides/magnesium-forms-compared/) explains each form in detail.

## What the evidence says

Magnesium supplements make the most difference when your diet is low in it: wholegrains, nuts, seeds, legumes and green vegetables are the main food sources. According to the US National Institutes of Health, forms that dissolve well in liquid, such as citrate, chloride and lactate, are absorbed better than oxide.

For **sleep**, a meta-analysis in older adults found people fell asleep about 17 minutes sooner, on low-quality evidence; see [magnesium for sleep](/en/guides/magnesium-for-sleep/). For **muscle cramps** in older adults, a 2020 Cochrane review concluded that magnesium is unlikely to provide meaningful protection. Neither use has an EU-authorised claim.
