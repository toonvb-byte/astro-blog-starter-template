## Zinc forms compared

| Form | Typical use | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bisglycinate | Tablets and capsules | Usually well tolerated |
| Picolinate | Capsules | Well absorbed; often pricier |
| Citrate | Tablets, effervescents | Well absorbed, mid price |
| Gluconate | Tablets, lozenges | Common in cold lozenges |
| Oxide | Cheap tablets, multivitamins | Most zinc per tablet; may be absorbed less well |
| Sulphate | Medicines, some supplements | Can upset the stomach |

## What the evidence says

Most people in Europe get enough zinc from meat, dairy, wholegrains and legumes; vegetarians and vegans need somewhat more because plant phytates reduce absorption. Supplements help most when intake is genuinely low.

For **colds**, a 2024 Cochrane review found that zinc may shorten an existing cold by about two days, on low-certainty evidence, and showed no clear benefit for prevention. The bigger practical risk is the opposite: high-dose products taken for weeks. Read [zinc and vitamin C for colds](/en/guides/zinc-vitamin-c-for-colds/) for the details.
