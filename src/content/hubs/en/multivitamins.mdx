      Most people eating a varied diet do not. A multivitamin can help with a
      restricted diet, low appetite or specific life stages, but it does not
      replace food and has not been shown to prevent chronic disease.

## Types of multivitamin compared

| Type | Typical difference | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard adult | Broad mix near 100% NRV | Check iron and vitamin D |
| Women | Iron and folic acid | Suits women who menstruate |
| Men / 50+ | Often less or no iron | Good default after menopause too |
| "Energy" formulas | High B vitamins, sometimes caffeine or ginseng | B vitamins only reduce tiredness if you were low |
| Gummies | Fewer nutrients, sugar | Often lack iron and some minerals |
| Prenatal | Folic acid, iodine, little vitamin A | Use instead of a standard multi in pregnancy |

## What the evidence says

In large trials, multivitamins have **not been shown to prevent cardiovascular disease, cancer or death** in well-nourished adults; in 2022 the US Preventive Services Task Force called the evidence insufficient and recommended against beta-carotene and vitamin E supplements for that purpose. Where multivitamins clearly help is in **filling real gaps**: restricted diets, low appetite in older age, or before and during pregnancy (folic acid).

Most of the useful decisions are on the label: see [how to read a supplement label](/en/guides/how-to-read-eu-supplement-label/) and the [maximum levels per country](/en/guides/supplement-maximum-levels-europe/).
