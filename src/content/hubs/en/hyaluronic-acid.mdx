## What the evidence says

Hyaluronic acid holds water in skin and joints, and the body makes its own. Whether swallowing it adds anything is less clear: a 2025 meta-analysis of seven randomised trials found statistically significant improvements in skin hydration, elasticity and wrinkle depth, but no clear effect on firmness, and the authors called for larger, more robust trials.

Many products combine it with collagen, whose own evidence for skin is disputed. Our guide on [collagen with magnesium or hyaluronic acid](/en/guides/collagen-magnesium-vs-hyaluronic-acid/) compares the combinations and what each may legally claim.
