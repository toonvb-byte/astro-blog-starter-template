## Creatine forms compared

| Form | What it is | Verdict from the evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monohydrate | The standard form used in almost all studies | The benchmark; cheapest per gram |
| Micronised monohydrate | Monohydrate with smaller particles | Mixes better; same effect |
| Creapure | A brand of monohydrate made in Germany | Same compound, documented purity testing |
| Hydrochloride (HCl) | Creatine bound to hydrochloric acid | Few studies; no proven advantage |
| Buffered (Kre-Alkalyn) | Creatine with altered pH | A 2012 trial found no advantage over monohydrate |

## What the evidence says

Creatine helps most in **short, intense efforts repeated with short breaks**: lifting, sprints, football, padel, HIIT. The International Society of Sports Nutrition calls monohydrate the most effective ergogenic supplement available for increasing high-intensity exercise capacity and lean mass during training. People who eat little meat or fish start with lower muscle creatine and tend to respond more.

Creatine works by filling your muscles' stores, so **timing matters less than taking it daily**. Our [creatine guide](/en/guides/creatine-monohydrate-guide/) covers dosing and myths in detail, and [creatine for women](/en/guides/creatine-for-women/) looks at the menopause and bone studies.
