takeaways:
  - >-
    Only **melatonin** has an authorised sleep claim in the EU: **1 mg close
    to bedtime** reduces the time it takes to fall asleep. In the **UK it is
    prescription-only**, so it is not an over-the-counter option.
  - >-
    **Magnesium, ashwagandha, valerian** and other herbs have **no authorised
    sleep claim**; the evidence ranges from modest to weak.
  - >-
    For insomnia that lasts for months, the 2023 **European Insomnia
    Guideline** recommends **cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT-I)** first, and
    does not recommend fast-release melatonin or herbal remedies.
  - >-
    "Sleep complexes" often combine many ingredients at **low or undisclosed
    doses**; check each one.

  - >-

faq:
  - q: What is the best supplement for sleep?
    a: >-
      In the UK, no over-the-counter supplement has an authorised sleep claim:
      melatonin, the only one with an authorised claim (1 mg close to bedtime), is
      prescription-only here. Its effect is modest anyway. For long-term
      insomnia, CBT-I is the recommended first treatment, and the NHS says a GP
      may offer it.
  - q: Does magnesium help you sleep?
    a: >-
      Possibly a little, mainly in people who get little magnesium from food.
      A meta-analysis in older adults found people fell asleep about 17 minutes
      sooner, on low-quality evidence. There is no authorised claim in the UK
      or EU.
  - q: Are herbal sleep remedies like valerian effective?
    a: >-
      The evidence is weak and inconsistent. The European Insomnia Guideline
      does not recommend phytotherapeutics for treating insomnia.

## Which supplement for which sleep problem

| Problem | Option with the best support | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trouble falling asleep | Melatonin, 1 mg about 30 minutes before bed | Modest: a few minutes sooner on average |
| Jet lag | Melatonin, 0.5 mg on travel days | authorised claim; helps shift the body clock |
| Waking during the night | No supplement with good evidence | Prolonged-release melatonin is a medicine option for over-55s |
| Stress keeps you awake | Ashwagandha (small effect), L-theanine (small trials) | No authorised claims; check who should avoid ashwagandha |
| Diet low in magnesium | Magnesium, up to 250 mg from supplements | Small, uncertain benefit |
| Long-term insomnia | CBT-I, not a supplement | First-line treatment in the European guideline |
