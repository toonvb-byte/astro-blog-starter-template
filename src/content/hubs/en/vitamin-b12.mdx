## Vitamin B12 forms compared

| Form | What it is | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cyanocobalamin | The standard synthetic form | Most studied, stable and cheap |
| Methylcobalamin | An active form found in the body | No proven absorption advantage; costs more |
| Hydroxocobalamin | The form used in injections | Mostly in medicines, rarely in supplements |
| Adenosylcobalamin | Another active form | Often combined with methylcobalamin; little extra evidence |

## What the evidence says

B12 comes almost only from animal foods, so a vegan diet without supplements or fortified foods eventually leads to deficiency. With age, stomach acid and intrinsic factor often fall, which makes B12 from food harder to absorb; supplements are absorbed better because the vitamin is not bound to food protein.

Absorption depends on the dose: at around 1–2 µg, about half is absorbed; at 500 µg about 2%, and at 1,000 µg about 1.3%, according to the US National Institutes of Health. Extra B12 reduces tiredness **only if you were low**; read our [guide to supplement labels](/en/guides/how-to-read-eu-supplement-label/) to compare doses and %NRV.
