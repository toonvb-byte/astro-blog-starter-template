  - >-
    Look for **mg of curcuminoids per daily dose**, not the weight of turmeric
    root or "extract": root powder contains only a few percent.
  - >-
    Compare that dose with **3 mg per kg of your body weight**, the EFSA
    acceptable daily intake.
  - >-
    Be extra careful with **piperine or enhanced-absorption** formulas
    (phytosome, micellar, colloidal): the same mg can mean far more in your
    blood.
  - >-
    Prefer brands that state **standardised curcuminoids** and test for heavy
    metals such as lead.
  - >-
    Remember that turmeric **in food** is not the issue; the concerns are about
    concentrated extracts.

## Turmeric products compared

| Type | What it is | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turmeric root powder | Ground root | Low curcuminoid content; similar to cooking turmeric |
| Extract, 95% curcuminoids | Standardised extract | Check mg of curcuminoids per daily dose |
| Extract + piperine | Curcumin with black pepper extract | Much higher absorption; watch the dose and medicines |
| Enhanced-absorption formulas | Phytosome, micellar or colloidal curcumin | Higher blood levels; regulators assess these case by case |
| Turmeric + ginger "complexes" | Blends with other plants and probiotics | Doses per ingredient often low or unclear |

## What the evidence says

Curcumin on its own is poorly absorbed, which is why most products add piperine or use special formulations. Trials in knee osteoarthritis suggest pain relief comparable to mild painkillers in some studies, but they are small and use concentrated extracts.

The safety picture has shifted. Italy recorded about 20 cases of hepatitis linked to turmeric supplements in 2018–2019 and now requires a label warning; France's food agency recorded over 100 adverse-event reports, 15 involving hepatitis, by 2021 and advises at most 153 mg of curcumin a day for a 60 kg adult. Germany's BfR says total curcumin should not exceed EFSA's acceptable daily intake, and in 2024 the UK's Committee on Toxicity found reasonable evidence of a link with liver injury.
