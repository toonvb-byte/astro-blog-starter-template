## Joint supplement ingredients compared

| Ingredient | Dose used in trials | What the evidence shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glucosamine sulphate | 1,500 mg/day | May reduce pain in mild to moderate knee osteoarthritis after about 6 months |
| Glucosamine hydrochloride | 1,500 mg/day | Mostly no benefit alone; mixed results with chondroitin |
| Chondroitin sulphate | 800–1,200 mg/day | Mixed results |
| MSM | Varies | Limited evidence |
| Collagen (peptides or type II) | 5–10 g/day or ~40 mg (UC-II) | Mixed trials; possible small benefit |
| Turmeric extract | Varies | Modest pain relief in small trials; liver warnings |

## What the evidence says

Results for glucosamine and chondroitin depend on the form, the dose and who paid for the study. Some trials of glucosamine sulphate in knee osteoarthritis found less pain after six months or more; large independent studies of other forms found no clear benefit over placebo. EFSA assessed joint claims for glucosamine and found that a cause-and-effect relationship had not been established.

In Spain, glucosamine sulphate and chondroitin sulphate are also sold as medicines, made to pharmaceutical standards; talk to your pharmacist about which fits you.
