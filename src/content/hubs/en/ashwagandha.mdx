## Ashwagandha extracts compared

| Type | Plant part | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KSM-66 | Root only | The most-studied branded extract; usually 300 or 600 mg |
| Sensoril | Root and leaf | Lower doses (125–250 mg); contains leaf |
| Shoden | Root and leaf | High withanolide content; contains leaf |
| Generic root powder | Root | Cheap; withanolide content often not stated |
| Gummies and blends | Varies | Dose per serving often low or unclear |

## What the evidence says

A 2021 review of seven trials with 491 adults found lower perceived stress, anxiety and cortisol than with placebo after 6–8 weeks; the trials were small and all run in India. A 2021 meta-analysis of five trials found a **small** effect on sleep, larger at 600 mg a day or more for at least eight weeks and in people with insomnia. The US National Institutes of Health notes that extracts differ widely and that there is almost no safety data beyond three months.

Regulators are cautious because of case reports of **liver injury**, possible effects on the thyroid and hormones, and missing safety data in pregnancy. Our [ashwagandha guide](/en/guides/ashwagandha-eu-legal-status/) has the country-by-country status table, updated as rules change.
